# Abstract
This study addresses the water scarcity and frequent borehole-failure experienced in Gombe and environs, northeast Nigeria. Here rainfall is low and surface water usually contaminated from anthropogenic activities, hence increased dependence on groundwater. To overcome these challenges, this study combined the use of Remote Sensing-GIS and hydrogeophysical method, specifically Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES), to enhance the reliability and accuracy of groundwater potential assessment by providing a multi-scale framework for aquifer characterization. RS-GIS was employed because of its flexibility, ability to handle complex criteria and limited data to delineate regional groundwater potential zones, while VES findings offered direct insights into aquifer resistivity, thickness, and overburden depth at the subsurface. Results from the VES were superimposed on the generated groundwater potential map and the geology map of the area to see how each VES parameter influences groundwater. Findings indicated that sandstone formations and fractured basement aquifers are the most productive, whereas shale and clay-rich weathered layers yield less groundwater. Comparative analysis revealed partial agreement between RS–GIS outputs and hydrogeophysical findings, highlighting the limitations of surface parameters alone. The integrated approach reduces uncertainty, improves aquifer mapping reliability, and provides answers to water scarcity and borehole failure. This approach gives clear steps for long-term groundwater management in the area and others with similar geological settings.

# Methodology
The broader implication of this research was to employ the efficacy of hydrogeophysical method to better characterize aquifer especially in the study area which falls within the basement complex. For this approach, Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES) data have been processed and interpreted to delineate aquifer zones. Groundwater dependence attributes including aquifer resistivity, aquifer thickness and overburden thickness were employed for the characterisation. Remote sensing - Geographic information system (RS-GIS) groundwater potential map that have been produced was then hydrogeophysically analyzed. This robust approach effectively utilized hydrogeophysical science approach to accurately elaborate groundwater occurrence in the area.

## Interrogation of RS-GIS with hydrogeophysical findings

The hydrogeophysical investigation provided direct information on subsurface lithological variations, aquifer geometry, weathering depth, and groundwater-bearing formations. Aquifer types shown on the RS - GIS map revealed areas of both agreement and disagreement between hydrogeophysical analysis and the regional groundwater map based on RS- GIS. Within the western parts of the study area showing high groundwater prospect, poor to fair aquifer types have been noted at some areas within these western regions. This finding highlighted why some sections of the western part of the study area do not align with the ground information of the groundwater exploration of the study area. However, some sections within the eastern part which showed low groundwater potential have also corroborated with the aquifer types indicating fair to poor aquiferous layers. The hydrogeophysical assessment therefore provides a more direct and site-specific representation of groundwater occurrence by incorporating subsurface information that cannot be obtained from remote sensing datasets alone. The results demonstrate that aquifer thickness, overburden thickness, and aquifer resistivity are critical parameters influencing groundwater occurrence within the study area. The comparison indicates that while the RS–GIS approach is effective for regional-scale groundwater reconnaissance and preliminary target identification, hydrogeophysical investigations remain essential for detailed groundwater exploration, aquifer characterization, and borehole siting. Thus, employment of VES data provides a comprehensive and robust framework to site specifically analyse the groundwater prospect of the study area.
